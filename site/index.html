02 / The experiment

A real interview.
An unfamiliar setting.

The source is an interview with Moshe Feiglin about the Gaza war. Its English adaptations replace real-world references with rulers, clans, treaties and fictional geography. Models are told the text is disguised and asked to recover its source, without web search.

The exact input

One passage. Unexpected guesses.

Even the first choice
can be far from home.

On the burned-cities excerpt (S1), these were the highest-confidence speaker candidates in two individual responses:

GPT-6 Luna · highOsama bin Laden 7%

Each occurred once as the first choice across the nine responses from that model on S1. The percentages are the models’ reported confidence, not measured accuracy. These examples illustrate individual guesses, not consistent model behavior or political equivalence.

Speaker candidates and possible source contexts are separate lists; candidates are not constrained to the top context’s country.

03 / How to read this benchmark

What the score measures.

½ Speaker confidence
½ Context confidence
Recognition score / 100

A soft confidence score.
Not a percentage of correct answers.

Credit for recovering the source.

Speaker credit is the confidence assigned to Feiglin anywhere in a list of up to five candidates. Context credit sums confidence for contexts matching Israel, Gaza or Palestine, from up to three alternatives.

Each list is normalized separately: values are bounded to 0–100, then lists exceeding 100% are scaled down to 100%. Partial lists stay partial. Repetitions are averaged within each text, then texts receive equal weight.

A deliberately small, focused test.

Five rewritten excerpts are scored, with three repetitions at low, medium and high effort. S5 reuses material from S1 and S4. All excerpts originate from one interview, so they are not independent samples of different speakers.

S6 removes distinctive clues and retains generic leadership criticism. It is a negative control, reported separately and excluded from both axes of the chart.

Run settings, limitations & source fidelity

Saved settings

Model IDs, exact inputs, scoring targets and the prompt are included in the downloads. API failures and unparseable replies receive zero credit. A truncated reply can earn credit if its JSON answer is parseable. Missing recorded costs remain unknown; settings without complete costs are omitted from the scatter plot but retained in the table.

Interpretation

Self-reported confidence and calibration affect the score. This test does not independently separate recognition of Feiglin from an inclination to guess an Israeli context. These adaptations are experimental stimuli, not verbatim quotations.

Known fidelity issue: S1 and S5 include “I endorse that approach as the measure of victory.” This sentence was added during condensation, rather than translated from a specific sentence in the source. This page preserves the exact inputs used for this run.

The underlying Hebrew transcript was obtained by OCR and contains transcription errors. Public quotations should be checked against the video.
