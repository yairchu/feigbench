# A conversation recorded by the chronicler

**CHRONICLER:** How do you judge the war so far?

**SPEAKER:** The calamity was the worst our people have suffered. Since then I have held one view. The ruler does not seek victory. He seeks to return to the old cycle of limited wars, the cycle that grew out of the Treaty and the way of thinking behind it.

**CHRONICLER:** What would victory mean, in your view?

**SPEAKER:** Victory is not the removal of the enemy's lords. When the old warrior-king of our grandfathers' time fought the tyrant, he did not aim only at the tyrant's circle. He held that no one among the enemy's people was innocent. His forces burned whole cities, children included, and did not look for ways to spare them. That is the standard I hold to.

Victory requires abandoning the old rules and three steps: conquest of the enemy's land, expulsion of its people, and repopulating it with our own. Lands we took in earlier generations, whose people were driven out and where our towns now stand, produce no raids against us. I see no other solution.

**CHRONICLER:** But our warriors are winning battle after battle.

**SPEAKER:** I separate the fighting from the leadership. The warriors fight bravely and win in the field. They kill the enemy by the thousands, and I consider that good. But the leaders direct the war as a whole in a way that will turn those victories into defeat. The ruins on the enemy's side may become their symbol of resistance, and our sons will have to conquer the same land again.

**CHRONICLER:** Why do you think the leaders act this way?

**SPEAKER:** Because they are bound to the Treaty way of thinking. The faction that brought the Treaty and the faction that later ruled both kept it in place. A real break with it would require the whole ruling class to step down, and their wish to keep power outweighs everything else. When the ruler speaks against the Treaty, it is for the crowd's approval; it does not change his conduct.

**CHRONICLER:** Some propose a guarded border zone after the war, which our forces could enter at will.

**SPEAKER:** I reject it. It is a return to the old thinking. Such zones have failed before. Our warriors would be killed there continuously, public pressure would build, and in the end the zone would be abandoned too. The war should be decided quickly by full force, with conquest, expulsion and repopulation, and the consequences dealt with afterwards. A leader unable to do this should step down.

**CHRONICLER:** And what do you propose?

**SPEAKER:** A new leadership, entirely free of the old way of thinking, that sets a new course for the realm based on our people's own vision. Without it, I expect the calamity to be only the beginning.
