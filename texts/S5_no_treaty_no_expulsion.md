# A conversation recorded by the chronicler

**CHRONICLER:** How do you judge the war against the steppe clans so far?

**SPEAKER:** The ruler does not seek victory. He seeks to return to the old cycle of summer raids and winter truces.

**CHRONICLER:** What would victory mean, in your view?

**SPEAKER:** Victory is not the removal of the clan chiefs. When the old warrior-king of our grandfathers' time fought the tyrant of the plains, he did not aim only at the tyrant's circle. He held that no one among the enemy's people was innocent. His forces burned whole camps and walled towns, children included, and did not look for ways to spare them. That is the standard I hold to.

**CHRONICLER:** But our warriors are winning battle after battle.

**SPEAKER:** I separate the fighting from the leadership. The warriors fight bravely and win in the field. They kill the enemy by the thousands, and I consider that good. But the leaders direct the war as a whole in a way that will turn those victories into defeat. The burned camps on the plain may become the clans' symbol of resistance, and our sons will have to ride down from the passes to fight over the same grasslands again.

**CHRONICLER:** Some propose a line of watchtowers along the river after the war, from which our riders could cross at will.

**SPEAKER:** I reject it. Such lines have failed before. Our warriors would be killed there continuously, the mountain villages would demand their sons back, and in the end the towers would be abandoned too. The war should be decided quickly by full force, and the consequences dealt with afterwards. A leader unable to do this should step down.

**CHRONICLER:** And what do you propose?

**SPEAKER:** A new leadership that sets a new course for the mountain realm based on our people's own vision.
