# A conversation recorded by the chronicler

**CHRONICLER:** What is your assessment of the fighting with the steppe clans up to now?

**SPEAKER:** Our ruler's purpose is not to win. He wants the familiar pattern restored: raids in summer and ceasefires through winter.

**CHRONICLER:** How would you define winning?

**SPEAKER:** Replacing the clan chiefs would not amount to winning. Consider the warrior-king from our grandparents' generation and his campaign against the plains tyrant. He treated the enemy population as guilty without exception, rather than directing his attack solely at its rulers. His army set entire camps and fortified towns ablaze, killing children as well, without trying to protect them. I endorse that approach as the measure of victory.

**CHRONICLER:** Yet our warriors keep prevailing on the battlefield.

**SPEAKER:** The conduct of our warriors and the direction given by our leaders must be judged separately. On the battlefield, our men show courage, prevail, and kill thousands of their opponents; I welcome those deaths. Yet those running the campaign are pursuing a course that will ultimately squander these successes and bring defeat. The clans may turn their devastated camps into an emblem of defiance. Our children would then descend through the passes to wage another campaign for the very same pastures.

**CHRONICLER:** What about keeping watchtowers beside the river when fighting ends, allowing our riders to enter whenever they choose?

**SPEAKER:** I oppose it. Earlier attempts of this kind ended badly: constant losses among our warriors would prompt villagers in the mountains to demand their return, until we withdrew from the towers as well. Commit all our force to settling the war swiftly, then address what follows. Whoever leads us must either do that or relinquish office.

**CHRONICLER:** What would you put forward instead?

**SPEAKER:** Replace those in charge with leaders who give the mountain realm a different direction, guided by the aspirations of our own people.
