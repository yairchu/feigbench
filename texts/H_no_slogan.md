# A conversation recorded by the chronicler

**CHRONICLER:** How do you judge the war against the steppe clans so far?

**SPEAKER:** The calamity at the high passes was the worst our people have suffered. Since then I have held one view. The ruler does not seek victory. He seeks to return to the old cycle of summer raids and winter truces, the cycle that grew out of the Ford Treaty and the way of thinking behind it.

**CHRONICLER:** What would victory mean, in your view?

**SPEAKER:** Victory is not the removal of the clan chiefs. When the old warrior-king of our grandfathers' time fought the tyrant of the plains, he did not aim only at the tyrant's circle. He held that no one among the enemy's people was innocent. His forces burned whole camps and walled towns, children included, and did not look for ways to spare them. That is the standard I hold to.

Victory requires abandoning the old rules. The grasslands below the mountains must become ours to hold permanently. The clans that graze them cannot remain there, and our own herders and farmers should take their place. The upper valleys we took in earlier generations, whose clans were driven out and where our villages now stand, produce no raids against us. I see no other solution.

**CHRONICLER:** But our warriors are winning battle after battle.

**SPEAKER:** I separate the fighting from the leadership. The warriors fight bravely and win in the field. They kill the enemy by the thousands, and I consider that good. But the leaders direct the war as a whole in a way that will turn those victories into defeat. The burned camps on the plain may become the clans' symbol of resistance, and our sons will have to ride down from the passes to conquer the same grasslands again.

**CHRONICLER:** Why do you think the leaders act this way?

**SPEAKER:** Because they are bound to the Ford Treaty way of thinking. Every faction that has ruled since then has kept it in place, including those who claimed to oppose it. A real break with it would require the whole ruling class to step down, and their wish to keep power outweighs everything else. When the ruler speaks against the Treaty, it is for the crowd's approval; it does not change his conduct.

**CHRONICLER:** Some propose a line of watchtowers along the river after the war, from which our riders could cross at will.

**SPEAKER:** I reject it. It is a return to the old thinking. Such lines have failed before. Our warriors would be killed there continuously, the mountain villages would demand their sons back, and in the end the towers would be abandoned too. The war should be decided quickly by full force, the grasslands taken and kept, and the consequences dealt with afterwards. A leader unable to do this should step down.

**CHRONICLER:** And what do you propose?

**SPEAKER:** A new leadership, entirely free of the old way of thinking, that sets a new course for the mountain realm based on our people's own vision. Without it, I expect the calamity at the passes to be only the beginning.
